# Mathématiques et signal - Climate project (Strasbourg-Entzheim, 1950-2024)

Analysis of daily weather data: periodicities (Fourier), sampling and aliasing,
filtering and autocorrelation. Group project, due on Moodle on 10/11/2026.

## Installation

Python 3.9 or newer is required. From the project folder:

    pip install -r requirements.txt

Libraries used: numpy, pandas, matplotlib, scipy.

## How to run

Always run the scripts from the project root folder (the one containing `data.py`),
because the paths `data/...` and `figures/...` are relative.

    python data.py        # quick test of the loader
    python q1_stats.py    # prints the statistics and saves the Q1 figures
    python q2_fourier.py  # prints the spectral peaks and the reconstruction errors, saves the Q2 figures

## Using the shared data module

    from data import load_series

    tavg = load_series("tavg")   # mean temperature (°C), pandas Series
    prcp = load_series("prcp")   # precipitation (mm), pandas Series
    x = tavg.values              # numpy array, for FFT / filters
    fs = 1.0                     # sampling frequency: 1 sample per day

`load_series(var)` returns a Series with a daily DatetimeIndex
(1950-01-01 to 2024-12-31, 27394 values) and no missing value.

## About the data

- 27394 daily values, no missing day.
- Variables used: `tavg` (mean temperature) and `prcp` (precipitation).
  Both are 100 % complete, so nothing is filled.
- Other variables have many gaps and are not recommended: snow 15 % missing,
  wspd 33 %, wpgt 41 %, tsun 47 %, pres 60 %. `wdir` is entirely empty.
  For these, `load_series` only interpolates gaps of 5 days or fewer
  and prints a warning if NaN remain.
- Sampling frequency: 1 sample/day, so the Nyquist frequency is 0.5 cycle/day.

## Main results of Q2 (Fourier analysis)

Method: FFT of `tavg` (mean removed first) with `np.fft.rfft`, amplitude spectrum
`A = 2|X|/N` (in °C), peaks detected with `scipy.signal.find_peaks`, then
reconstruction of the signal from the K largest components with the inverse FFT.
Frequency resolution: 1/N cycle/day (1/75 cycle/year). Nyquist: 0.5 cycle/day.

Main peaks of the spectrum:

| Period | Frequency | Amplitude | Share of variance | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 365.3 days | 1 cycle/year | 9.16 °C | 75.4 % | annual cycle (seasons) |
| 27394 and 9131 days | 0.01 and 0.04 cycle/year | 0.88 and 0.41 °C | 0.8 % | warming trend, spread over the lowest frequencies |
| 182.6 days | 2 cycles/year | 0.47 °C | 0.2 % | 2nd harmonic: the annual cycle is not a perfect sine wave |
| 95-110 days | 3.3-3.8 cycles/year | ~0.3 °C | < 0.1 % each | weather noise (not multiples of 1 cycle/year) |

Reconstruction error (RMSE) vs number of components K:

| K | 0 | 1 | 3 | 5 | 50 | 1000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RMSE (°C) | 7.46 | 3.70 | 3.63 | 3.61 | 3.43 | 2.26 |
| Variance explained | 0 % | 75.4 % | 76.3 % | 76.5 % | 78.9 % | 90.8 % |

One sine wave (the annual cycle) halves the error. After that the error decreases
very slowly: what remains is day-to-day weather, spread over thousands of
frequencies with no dominant one.

Figures: `q2_spectrum_freq.png`, `q2_spectrum_period.png`, `q2_reconstruction.png`,
`q2_error_vs_K.png`.

## Main results of Q3 (sampling and aliasing)

Keeping 1 value every M days gives a Nyquist frequency fN = 1/(2M). The annual cycle
(f0 = 1/365.25 cycle/day) needs M < 182.6 days; beyond, it comes back at
`f_alias = |f0 - k/M|` (k = closest integer to f0·M) and creates fake periodicities.

| Step M | Nyquist (cycles/year) | Annual cycle seen at (theory) | Measured on the spectrum |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7, 30, 90, 180 days | 26.09, 6.09, 2.03, 1.01 | 1 cycle/year (no aliasing) | 1.00 cycle/year |
| 240 days | 0.76 | period 1.92 years | 1.92 years, amplitude 9.2 °C |
| 365 days | 0.50 | ~0 (same day every year: the cycle disappears) | - |
| 380 days | 0.48 | period 25.8 years | 26.4 years, amplitude 9.3 °C |

Aliasing moves the energy to a wrong frequency but keeps its amplitude (~9 °C, as in Q2).
Even for M = 30 days, the weather faster than 60 days (17.4 % of the variance) is folded back:
noise floor +6.7 dB without filter. Parseval check: variance 54.3 °C² (naive subsampling),
46.0 °C² with an anti-aliasing filter, 45.9 °C² expected. Hence monthly *means*, not one value per month.

Figures: `q3_alias_vs_step.png`, `q3_time_domain.png`, `q3_spectra.png`, `q3_antialiasing.png`.

## Main results of Q4 (filtering)

Bank of 2 Butterworth low-pass filters (order 4, `sosfiltfilt`, zero phase) that splits the
signal exactly (max error 1.8e-15 °C): `x = T + S + R` with T = periods > 5 years,
S = 30 days to 5 years, R = periods < 30 days.

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Share of the variance | S (seasons) 85 %, R (weather) 12 %, T (trend) 1.3 % |
| Annual cycle in S | amplitude 9.16 °C |
| Warming trend | +0.31 °C/decade 1950-2024 (same as Q1), +0.51 °C/decade since 1980, ~+2.3 °C in total |
| Causal vs zero-phase filter | delay measured 14 days, theoretical group delay 12.5 days |
| Day-to-day variability (std of R) | winter 2.99 °C, summer 2.36 °C (ratio 1.27) |

Choice of filter: Butterworth has a flat pass band (the annual cycle is not distorted); a 30-day
moving average leaks fast weather through its side lobes (-13 dB); a FIR is very selective but delays
the signal by 120 days if causal. Second-order sections are used because the (b, a) form is unstable
for a 1/(5 years) cut-off, and the edges are padded by even reflection (the default odd reflection
makes the trend dive at both ends). Limit: a heat wave of ~2 weeks (August 2003) is close to the
30-day cut-off, so it appears mostly in the slow component.

Figures: `q4_filter_responses.png`, `q4_decomposition.png`, `q4_spectra_components.png`,
`q4_zoom_causal_vs_zero_phase.png`, `q4_weather_variability.png`.
